Iron Vale · identity exploration · one of six

Scroll & Parchment

Today every surface in Iron Vale wears the same coat. A settings form, a quest offer, a stats table and a combat log are all the same black panel with the same gold hairline, and sameness is most of what reads as machine-made. This option splits the world in two by material. The world stays dark: town, roads, combat, the givers' houses at dusk. But the moment the player opens something to read or to set, a scroll unrolls over it, and the value scheme inverts: dark ink on warm vellum, the way you actually read for two minutes at a time. Gold gets its meaning back because it never leaves the dark side.

The palette is not invented. The field colour is #d8cfa8, which is already --ink in static/style.css. Iron Vale has been writing in parchment for its whole life; this just enlarges that colour from a hairline of type into a plane and moves the ink to the other side.

01The material

Every value below is a hard step. No smooth ramps, no blur, no border-image, nothing a CRT would not have shown you. The fibre is real 1px banding at a 5px and 8px pitch; the foxing is five hand-placed stains, never a noise texture.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7
  1. 1Finial. The dowel runs 13px past the sheet on each side and is banded in iron at its waist. It is the one place the dark world's metal survives onto the light side, and it is what tells you the sheet is an object and not a page.
  2. 2Roller. 20px, six hard bands of wood plus two grain passes at a 11px and 29px pitch so the grain never lines up with itself. The roller is the title bar — it replaces .win-title, which cannot survive here (see 08).
  3. 3Curl. Four steps of shadow, 7px total, where the sheet leaves the dowel. Mirrored at the foot. This single detail is what makes the field read as cloth rather than as a light-coloured div.
  4. 4Field. #d8cfa8 flat. Not a gradient. All the life comes from the three layers on top of it.
  5. 5Fibre. 1px of #7c6a49 at 7.5% every 5px across, and at 5% every 8px down. Laid lines. At 100% it is invisible until you look for it, which is the point.
  6. 6Foxing. Five stains, hand-placed at 18/12, 74/31, 88/76, 33/63, 55/92, each a two-step hard radial. They are in fixed positions so every scroll in the game ages identically — a repeated mark reads as a printing, a random one reads as a bug.
  7. 7Aged edge. 2px of #a4926a then 3px of #c3b489 down both long edges, on top of the fibre. The sheet is darker where hands hold it.

02Settings, as a scroll

Real content, from static/js/misc.js. Three tabs become three vellum tags tied along the roller and hanging off the sheet's left edge, so the selected one slides out to meet you instead of latching down like a key. No row of buttons anywhere on the sheet: units are a choice underlined in rubric, sound is an inked mark, ambition is the tier that has been struck, and the one committing action is a wax seal.

It is the only control that keeps its travel in the y axis, because a bookmark is the one thing on a sheet that hangs rather than sits. Pressed is 3px down and a shade darker; nothing else moves.

What did not survive. There is no parchment equivalent of .btn.green or .btn.danger. On a light field, semantic colour has to live in the ink, not the face — green becomes leaf #3d6a2a ink on plain vellum, danger becomes rubric. That is a smaller vocabulary than the dark side has, and it is a real loss.

05The unroll

Four frames, steps(1, end), about 180ms end to end. No opacity, no easing, no blur. A modal that fades is software; a scroll that falls open is a thing that happened in the room. The content is painted with the sheet as it passes, never before it — the paper arrives first and the words come down with it.

07The phone

Scrolls are tall and narrow, which is a gift at 390px. The head dowel pins to the top of the viewport and the sheet runs under it, curl shadow and all, so the title bar is the roller and costs no extra chrome. The tags come off the left edge and lie across the head as a row of pennants. The dark world keeps the header and the dock; the scroll owns everything between them.

08What it changes, what it keeps, what it costs

Straight, no selling.

What it changes

  • Reading and configuring surfaces get their own material. Everything else — town, roads, combat, the givers' houses, the Hall — stays exactly as dark as it is.
  • The panel title stops being a notch chip and becomes the roller. That is one fewer floating element and one more physical one.
  • Tab rows stop being key caps. They become tags on a cord, which is diegetic and reads at a glance because the selected one is a different shape, not just a different colour.
  • Buttons on a sheet become stamps, seals, ribbons and marks. There is exactly one seal per sheet, so the committing action is unmistakable without being loud.
  • Opening something becomes an event with four frames and a sound, instead of a modal that appears.

What it keeps

  • The whole palette. --ink becomes the field, --red becomes the rubric, --dim and --dim-readable get direct twins. Nothing is imported from outside the game.
  • Hard pixel steps everywhere. No radius, no blur, no gradient a CRT would not have shown. The drop shadow is still a flat offset block.
  • The real fonts at their native sizes — 10, 12, 14, 16, and it finally gives quanta-strike-20 a job as the scroll's lead line.
  • The sprites, unchanged. ks_seal and icon_coin were drawn warm enough that they sit on vellum without a single pixel edited.
  • The vignette. See below.

What it costs

  • Gold dies on this field. --gold #c9a24b on #d8cfa8 is about 1.5:1. Every gold accent on a sheet has to become --gilt #8a6a1e (~4.4:1). Practically: gold stops being "important" and starts meaning "the dark world". That is a strong rule, but it is a rule the whole app now has to keep.
  • .win-title cannot survive. It works by punching a hole in the panel border and filling it with var(--bg). There is no dark to notch into on a scroll. Any screen that uses the notch has to be re-headed on the roller.
  • .btn is a second full system, not a variant. Gold-bright text on #2e2a1c with 0 4px 0 #3a2c10 is a dark blob on vellum. The stamp family is genuinely new CSS, and every call site inside a reading surface has to be re-classed. This is the biggest bill.
  • Three new ink tokens, minimum — --iron-ink, --ink-mid, --ink-dim — plus --leaf and --lake for semantic text. Every component that hardcodes var(--ink) has to be audited for which side of the world it is on, and that is most of a 2889-line stylesheet.
  • Semantic colour narrows. --green #7ab55c is 2.1:1 on vellum and --blue #6aa0c8 is 2.4:1. Both need darker twins, and the "coloured face" button variants (.btn.green, .btn.danger) have no parchment equivalent at all — the colour has to move into the ink.
  • The bold strike gets benched. quanta-strike-12-bold was drawn to hold up light-on-dark. Dark-on-light it clots at 12px. Emphasis on a sheet has to come from rubric and from the 20 strike, not from weight.
  • The desktop column stops filling its width. Vellum argues for a ~284-340px reading measure, well under the 880px #app. Better reading, emptier screen. Either the scroll gets a companion in the margin or desktop accepts side space.
  • The vignette still works, and changes meaning. At rgba(0,0,0,0.45) it greys the sheet's corners, which on parchment reads as age and candlelight rather than as CRT falloff — arguably better. But it does cost about 8% of the field's brightness at the corners, so if a scroll ever fills the viewport the vignette should drop to ~0.28 over it. That is a conditional, and conditionals are how design systems rot. Worth naming now.
The honest summary. This is not a reskin, it is a second half of the design system, and it costs roughly a button system, a token layer and one hard rule about gold. What it buys is that a settings sheet and a combat log stop looking like the same object, and that the thing you stare at for two minutes is finally the highest-contrast thing on the screen.
