Iron Vale — identity exploration — option: frame & hanging

MATERIAL CHROME

A panel is an object hung in a room, not a rectangle drawn on a void.
What reads as machine-made is not the black and not the gold. It is that every panel is an unattached rectangle with one uniform border and a label pasted over the top line. Nothing holds it up. Nothing casts on it. It has no back.

This study keeps the palette, the pixel fonts, the straight-down key shadow and the vignette, and changes one thing: the frame is made of something, and something holds it there. Three materials, one lamp, and a corner and a title that a person had to decide.
00

The lamp, and the rule it makes

One source. Upper-left, forty-five degrees, and it never moves for the whole app. Every highlight lives on a top or a left face. Every occlusion falls down and to the right. Every step is hard — 1px or 2px, blur zero. No ramp anywhere; if a surface needs to look curved it gets more steps, not softer ones.

What this does not do. It does not paint a wall behind every screen. The background stays --bg exactly as it ships. The wall is asserted only by the fasteners — a rivet is enough to tell you there is something behind the plate. The one exception is Set Stone, which cannot exist without the wall it is set into; section 03 shows how much wall that actually needs.
01

Bolted iron

Hammered plate, bolted flat. Rivets at the four corners and at the mid-span of the long edges, because a plate this wide would sag between two fixings and a smith would know it. A raised outer flange carries the fasteners; the content sits in a field routed below the flange, so text is never on the same plane as the frame. Title on a brass tag riveted across the top edge. Where it belongs: the Undercroft, the forge, anything Hesk or Grunhilda owns.

02

Hung oak

Three planks with the joins showing, pegged at the corners, hung from two nails and tilted 0.35° — not enough to notice, enough to stop reading as a print-out. The title is cut into the top plank: a dark stroke with a lit lower-right lip, because the far wall of a chisel groove is the one facing the lamp. Two corners are chipped; the exposed wood under a fresh chip is paler than the weathered face. Content sits in a routed pocket, never on bare grain. Where it belongs: the notice board, quest offers, doctrines — anything Fenn or the town posts.

03

Set stone

The third is chosen to argue the widest, so it is the one that isn't hung at all. A slab mortared into a wall casts nothing outward — the wall casts onto it. So the recess inverts: shade on the upper-left inner edge, lamp on the lower-right. If the language holds at both extremes (an object stuck on a wall, and a hole cut into one) it is a system rather than a skin. Title chiselled straight in; corners taken by chisel chips, not fixings. Where it belongs: the Hall of Records, the Colosseum, the Road — the permanent places.

04

The title, five ways

Today the title is a cutout of --bg laid over the border line. It works, and it is the single element that most says “a machine put a label here.” Five physical answers, side by side at the same size, on the same plate.

The rule that keeps five from being chaos. The title treatment is not decoration — it encodes permanence. Riveted = fixed by the world. Hung = fixed by you, and changeable. Carved = part of the thing. Stitched = ceremonial. Sealed = sworn. A player never has to learn this; they just stop being surprised by which panels they can change.

06

Nesting — where heavy chrome usually dies

Two rules, and they are the whole answer. One: at most one material change per nesting level, and the inner surface is always recessed relative to the outer — never raised, or the eye loses which one is the container. Two: hardware does not nest. Rivets, pegs, chains and seals live on the outermost frame only; an inner panel gets a carved title and a recess and nothing else. Depth costs 6px a side, not another twelve rivets.

08

The phone case — 390px

Heavy frames eat horizontal space, and the phone is where the game actually gets played. The answer is not to shrink the frame proportionally — a 1px rivet is a smudge. It is to drop parts of the vocabulary and keep the ones that carry the identity:

▸ Flange 16px → 7px; field inset 14px → 10px. Total 20px → 11px a side.
▸ Fasteners: corners only. Mid-span rivets and pegs are dropped below 720px.
▸ Titles: carved and stamped only. Tags, chains, banners and seals all overhang, and overhang is exactly what a 390px screen cannot spare.
▸ Nesting capped at one level. The second level becomes a hairline rule.
▸ The board keeps its tilt and its nails. They cost 0px of width and they are most of the charm.

09

What this costs

Counted from the real stylesheet, not estimated. static/style.css is 2,889 lines; 79 of its rules declare both a border and a background, and about 34 of those are containers rather than key states or canvases. Those 34 are the ones that would each need a material assigned.

WhatThe honest number
Panel-ish classes needing a material ~34 containers. .win alone is touched by 22 rules across the overlay, dev console, compendium, logger and two breakpoints — so “just restyle .win” is already twenty-two edits before the other thirty-three classes. Realistically only three need a material by hand: .win, .pip-panel, .prog-card. The rest inherit from a data-material attribute on the screen root, which is the only way this stays maintainable.
Information density Yes, it hurts, and by a measurable amount: +14px a side for iron, +30px for stone, +32px for oak, against the shipped 6px. On the 880px desktop column that is 3–7% of the width. On a 320px phone, in the thin variant, it is +5px a side — roughly two characters of the 12px strike per line. The Hall's calendar grid and the Undercroft's map are the two screens where this genuinely bites; both should stay .win.tight in iron and skip the pocket.
The --edge-lit / --edge-shade contract This is the real breakage. Today every raised surface — menus, toasts, speech bubbles, the pixel-select popup — shares one neutral bevel: border-color: var(--edge-lit) var(--edge-shade) var(--edge-shade) var(--edge-lit) on --surface-raised, with a straight 4px 4px drop. Five rules use --edge-lit; four use --surface-raised. That contract says “this floats above everything and belongs to no place.” Material Chrome does not break it — it promotes it. A menu genuinely does float above the room, so the neutral bevel becomes the marker of the layer that is not part of the world: popups, toasts, overlays, the dev console. Everything that is a place gets a material. The cost is that the two systems must never touch: a menu rendered inside an oak pocket would read as a floating card glued to a plank, and that is worse than today. Practically that means overlays keep rendering at the app root, which they already do.
New tokens 21 new custom properties (7 per material) plus one --oct clip-path. No existing token changes value. Three of the un-tokenized hexes already in the stylesheet (#6b5426, #4a3d20, #3a2c10) get names for the first time as the brass family.
New markup Fasteners are empty <i> elements — 4 to 6 per framed panel, 2 nested nodes each. On the Hall's Body tab that is about 30 extra DOM nodes. Nothing is an image, nothing is a font, nothing is an animation. Total added CSS is roughly 180 lines.
Names already taken Found the hard way while building this page: the obvious material vocabulary collides with classes the app already owns. .chip is used 13 times in style.css as the small badge on quest offers, and .plate is already .bld .plate on the town buildings. Naming the corner-wear element .chip silently collapsed every offer badge on this page to zero width before it was caught. Any implementation needs a prefix — .mat-plate, .mat-wear — not bare material nouns.
What it does not cost No new art files. No new fonts (it uses the two already-shipped-but-unwired strikes, 18 and 20, for material headings). No JS beyond what already renders sprites. No change to the vignette, the key press behaviour, the 4px drop, the straight-down key shadow, or the phone breakpoint.
What I would cut first if this were half-built. The stone wall. It is the only element that requires a backdrop, the only one that inverts the lamp, and the one most likely to fight a future chart or calendar. Iron and oak carry the whole argument on their own; stone is what proves it is a system, and a proof can be deferred.
The one thing I would not compromise. The title. Four of the ten details in this study are title treatments, and if only one change ever ships it should be that the panel name stops being a cutout floating on the border line and starts being cut into or fixed onto the thing it names. It is two CSS declarations, it costs nothing, and it is most of the difference between “generated” and “made.”
Material Chrome — one of six identity options explored in parallel. This page adds no files to the app and changes nothing that ships.
