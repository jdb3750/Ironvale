The pick: A. A survives on ink, on paper, on gold, at 16×16, and it keeps the game's existing gold. C stays in the kit as the title face — see section 07.

04Stop shouting
    roles as different objects, not one object at different sizes

  
The app currently applies text-transform:uppercase and letter-spacing:1px
  to titles, buttons, chips, labels, tabs and section heads alike. Uppercase is a tool: it works on a 10px label
  because there are three words of it. On a sentence it removes ascenders and descenders, which are most of what you read
  a word by, and it makes everything sound like the same person shouting.

What breaks on a phone, and the rule

      

        
    
          
  • The 4× mark is 544px. Anything above 2× is desktop-only; the gate uses the stacked
            lockup at 2× (126px wide) instead.
    • 
          
  • Drop caps drop to 1× (24px), which is only two lines of 12px body. On any card with
            fewer than three lines the drop cap is dropped entirely and the first three words go gold.
    • 
          
  • Cutstone titles need no change — the longest one, “THE IRON LEDGER”, is 173px.
    • 
          
  • The 18/20 strikes are what make the phone work: a lede at 18px reads as a heading on a 390px
            column, where the same words at 26px PressStart wrap to three lines.
    • 
        

      

    

  







  
—What this changes, keeps and costs


  

    

      
What it changes

      
    
        
  • Iron Vale gets a wordmark. There is none today.
    • 
        
  • The app icon becomes a drawn seal instead of a square in a square, and the favicon is drawn at 16×16
          rather than scaled to it.
    • 
        
  • Screen titles stop being PressStart and become drawn art; panel titles drop to 10px with 3px tracking.
          Those are two different objects now instead of one at two sizes.
    • 
        
  • Body copy goes mixed case. Buttons, chips and the 10px labels stay uppercase.
    • 
        
  • Two font files that already ship get used, and the display ladder gains its missing rungs at 18 and 20.
    • 
        
  • Long copy gets an illuminated capital on four surfaces: giver dialog, doctrine cards, almanac pages, keepsakes.
    • 
        
  • Headline numbers get a drawn ledger set with a slashed zero and a flagged one.
    • 
      

    

    

      
What it keeps

      
    
        
  • Every panel and key geometry, untouched: .win border, both rings, the 6px drop,
          .btn bevel colours and 4px key floor.
    • 
        
  • The whole palette. The three drawn faces use --gold-bright, --gold,
          #6b5426 and #3a2c10 only — all four are already in the stylesheet, two of them
          as un-tokenized recurring values.
    • 
        
  • All four active Quanta strikes at their native sizes. Nothing is resized away from its strike.
    • 
        
  • The size-bound doctrine from DESIGN.md:129-133. The two new roles are two more
          size-bound strikes, not fluid type.
    • 
        
  • The sprite pipeline. Every drawn thing here is { p, r } row-strings rendered to a
          canvas by the same 18 lines pixel.js already has.
    • 
        
  • The gameboy read: hard pixels, no smoothing, no anti-aliasing, one light source top-left.
    • 
      

    

  


  

    
What it costs — the honest list

    
    
      
  • No new font files are needed. Not one. The wordmark, the three faces, the drop caps, the
        numerals and every drawn title on this page are sprites — row strings in JS, rendered to canvas. The only
        font work is the two @font-face blocks for strikes already sitting in static/fonts/.
        Total added bytes over the wire: 18,632, both files already committed.
    • 
      
  • The drawn faces are 68 glyph masks of source. A–Z in three faces plus 0–9 in one, at
        roughly 16 lines each. That is real code sitting in a JS file — call it 1,400 short lines. It compresses
        to almost nothing and it never needs a build step, but somebody has to look at it in a diff.
    • 
      
  • Mixed case is a copy rewrite, and it is the biggest cost here. Turning off
        text-transform:uppercase does not produce mixed case — it produces whatever case the string was
        written in. Most of the app's strings were authored knowing they would be shouted, so a good many are already
        capitalised correctly, but the sweep has to be done by hand across giver.js, hall.js,
        town.js, misc.js, ranch.js, colosseum.js and
        dungeon.js — and every string has to be read in place, because “SWORN” is a chip
        and stays, while “THE OMENS” is a heading and goes. There is no safe automatic version of this.
    • 
      
  • 144 paired family+size declarations is what a new role actually means. The stylesheet
        writes font-family and font-size together every time, by design. Adding
        --font-lede and --font-herald is two token pairs, but every place that adopts
        a new role is a two-line edit, and the pairs cannot drift apart or the strike blurs. That discipline is the
        reason the app looks crisp; it is also the reason a type change is never a one-line change here.
    • 
      
  • Drawn titles are images, so they carry no text. Each canvas needs an
        aria-label and the underlying string must stay in the DOM for search, selection and screen readers
        — the same thing spriteTag() already does not do. If a title needs to be dynamic
        (“Doctrines of Iron-Bell”), it cannot be drawn: it gets the 10px letterspaced treatment and
        only the fixed part is art. About a third of the app's panel titles interpolate a name, so a third of them will
        never be carved.
    • 
      
  • Five drop caps is not a set. Twenty-six is, and the four ornament variants multiply that
        by however many moods you want. Ship the four surfaces listed above, or draw the other twenty-one.
    • 
    

  


  

    
Where this meets the other five options

    
    
      
  • Frame materials. The carved title in section 07 assumes there is a material to cut into. If that option
        lands, this one should adopt its surface colour and its light direction rather than shipping its own stone.
        The two are the same decision seen from opposite sides.
    • 
      
  • Nine-slice tile kit. The hanging tag is a small nine-slice waiting to happen; the banner ribbon is
        currently CSS clip-path and should be tiles if that kit exists.
    • 
      
  • Palette studies. Vale Chisel is drawn entirely from four existing gold values. Any palette shift
        rewrites the mark's palette map — four hex strings in one object — and nothing else.
    • 
      
  • Micro-detail. The inlay channel, the struck baseline rule and the seal's ring are micro-detail; if that
        option has a house rule for 1px highlights, this should follow it.
    • 
    

  


  

    
The surprises, reported rather than designed around

    
    
      
  • vt323.woff2 has an @font-face at style.css:2-5 and is applied to
        nothing at all — it is loaded and then never named again. The 18 and 20 strikes are the reverse: files with
        no rule. So the repo ships one rule with no user and two users with no rule. Worth deleting the VT323 block in
        the same pass that adds the other two.
    • 
      
  • #3a2c10 is doing two unrelated jobs: it is the key floor shadow on .btn and it is
        the .pixel-title text-shadow. If display type stops being PressStart, that second use disappears and
        the value becomes a single-purpose token. Worth naming.
    • 
      
  • The heaviest reason today's titles read as generated is not the font — it is that
        .win-title, .btn, .chip, .omens-title,
        .counsel-label and the tab buttons all carry text-transform:uppercase plus
        letter-spacing:1px. Six different kinds of object with one typographic voice. Fixing that costs
        no new assets at all and is probably worth doing even if none of the drawn work ships.
    •
